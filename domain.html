01

Stochastic Systems & Non-Equilibrium Statistical Physics

Classical mechanics studies idealized, isolated trajectories. But real systems—from colloidal particles buffeted by thermal water molecules to deep neural networks navigating non-convex loss landscapes under stochastic mini-batch noise—are fundamentally governed by fluctuations.

My work models these systems through non-equilibrium statistical mechanics and continuous-time stochastic differential equations (SDEs). Using the framework of Itô and Stratonovich calculus, we analyze how dissipation, thermal kicks, and microscopic entropy production give rise to macroscopic order. [†]Jarzynski's Equality: \(\langle e^{-\beta W} \rangle = e^{-\beta \Delta F}\), connecting non-equilibrium work measurements to equilibrium free energy differences.

$$\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}_t = -\nabla V(\mathbf{X}_t)\,\mathrm{d}t + \sqrt{2\beta^{-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{W}_t \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \partial_t p(\mathbf{x},t) = \nabla \cdot \Big(p(\mathbf{x},t)\nabla V(\mathbf{x}) + \beta^{-1}\nabla p(\mathbf{x},t)\Big)$$

Langevin SDEs & Itô Calculus

Formulating continuous-time Markovian processes with stochastic differentials. Exploring the transition between underdamped (inertial) and overdamped regimes, and the stability of symplectic numerical integrators (BAOAB, Euler-Maruyama).

Thermodynamics of Learning

Treating gradient descent as an open thermodynamic system relaxing in an energetic loss landscape. Mini-batch gradient covariance acts as an effective temperature facilitating barrier-crossing out of sharp non-generalizing minima.

Fokker-Planck Probability Currents

Tracking probability distribution densities under drift and diffusion fields. Investigating stationary states, Gibbs-Boltzmann distributions, and Non-Equilibrium Steady States (NESS) where non-zero probability currents circulate continuously.

Dynamical Game Theory

Analyzing multi-agent strategic interactions as coupled stochastic differential systems. Formulating Nash equilibria as fixed points of coupled Fokker-Planck equations where individual agent optimization yields emergent macro-dynamics.

02

Modern Machine Learning & AI: Physics-Informed Dynamics

Standard deep learning treats neural networks as unconstrained, black-box function approximators that rely on massive training sets to prevent unphysical extrapolation. In physical systems, this is unacceptable: mass must be conserved, energy cannot spontaneously emerge, and Navier-Stokes momentum must be respected.

My research focuses on embedding physical conservation laws, differential operators, and continuous-time dynamics directly into backpropagation graphs.

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{PINN}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N_d}\sum_{i=1}^{N_d} |u_\theta(x_i, t_i) - u_i|^2 + \lambda \int_\Omega \left|\partial_t u_\theta + (u_\theta \cdot \nabla)u_\theta + \nabla p_\theta - \nu \nabla^2 u_\theta\right|^2 \mathrm{d}\Omega$$

Continuous-Time Diffusion Models

Score-based generative models framed as forward-backward SDEs. Perturbing complex data distributions to Gaussian noise via Ornstein-Uhlenbeck drift, then reversing time using neural score estimators \(\nabla_x \log p_t(x)\).

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)

Solving forward and inverse partial differential equations by encoding PDE residuals directly into backpropagation graphs. Utilizing automatic differentiation to compute exact derivatives without mesh discretization.

Fourier Neural Operators (FNO)

Learning infinite-dimensional mappings between function spaces rather than discrete vectors. Fourier Neural Operators parameterize kernel integrals in frequency space, allowing zero-shot resolution transfer.

Representation Learning & Symmetries

Joint embedding architectures respecting physical conservation laws (momentum, mass, energy) and geometric Lie groups (\(SO(3)\), \(SE(3)\)).

03

Quantum Theory, Path Integrals & The Higgs Mechanism

As part of my academic minor in Quantum Technology at the Indian Institute of Science, I study quantum mechanics, path integrals, and gauge field theories. A central marvel of modern theoretical physics is spontaneous symmetry breaking and the Higgs mechanism.

In a complex scalar field \(\phi\), when the mass parameter squared is negative (\(-\mu^2 < 0\)), the potential takes on the famous "Mexican hat" shape:

$$V(\phi) = -\mu^2 |\phi|^2 + \lambda |\phi|^4 \quad\implies\quad \langle 0 | \phi | 0 \rangle = \frac{v}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu^2}{2\lambda}}$$

While the Lagrangian remains invariant under \(U(1)\) phase rotations, the physical vacuum state chooses a specific phase, spontaneously breaking the symmetry. When coupled to a gauge field through the covariant derivative \(D_\mu = \partial_\mu - i g A_\mu\), the massless Goldstone mode is "eaten" by the gauge boson, generating the mass term:

$$|D_\mu \phi|^2 \supset g^2 |\phi|^2 A_\mu A^\mu \xrightarrow{\phi \to (v + h)/\sqrt{2}} \frac{1}{2} M_A^2 A_\mu A^\mu, \qquad M_A = g v$$

Feynman Path Integrals & QFT

Connecting quantum transitions to functional integrals over all possible field histories. Wick rotation (\(t \to -i\tau\)) mapping quantum field theory directly onto Euclidean statistical mechanics partition functions.

Open Quantum Systems

Describing system-bath interactions where environmental coupling induces decoherence and dissipation. Solving Lindblad master equations for quantum state fidelity in realistic, noisy quantum hardware.

Quantum Computing & Circuits

Quantum state tomography, unitary operators, quantum Fourier transform (QFT), variational quantum eigensolvers (VQE), and error mitigation strategies in NISQ-era devices.

Casimir Effect & Vacuum Polarization

The quantum vacuum is not empty; zero-point fluctuations of the electromagnetic field produce a physically measurable attractive force between uncharged conducting boundaries.

04

Computational Physics, Fluid Flow & High-Performance Systems

Fluid dynamics represents one of the most intellectually compelling arenas of non-linear physics. Incompressible fluid flow is governed by the continuous balance of momentum and incompressibility:

$$\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p + \nu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u}, \qquad \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$$

Taking the curl of the momentum equation yields the vorticity transport equation:

$$\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{\omega}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\boldsymbol{\omega} = (\boldsymbol{\omega} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} + \nu \nabla^2 \boldsymbol{\omega}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\omega} = \nabla \times \mathbf{u}$$

Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS)

Resolving the full spectrum of turbulent scales down to the Kolmogorov dissipation scale \(\eta = (\nu^3 / \epsilon)^{1/4}\) without empirical sub-grid modeling.

GPU Acceleration & CUDA

Writing high-throughput parallel kernels, coalesced global memory access patterns, shared memory cache utilization, and multi-stream concurrent execution on NVIDIA hardware.

Pseudo-Spectral Methods

Transforming continuous spatial derivatives into algebraic multiplications via Fast Fourier Transforms (\(\partial_x \to ik_x\)), achieving exponential convergence for smooth periodic flows.

Autograd Memory Scaling Analysis

Benchmarking the memory overhead of autograd backward calculation graphs against lean, memory-efficient classical finite-difference stencils in large-scale simulation grids.
