IISc FLAME Lab // Optimal Transport FLAME Lab Research

Flow-Matching vs Diffusion for Sparse-Sensor Turbulence Reconstruction

Reconstructing turbulent velocity and vorticity fields from sparse physical sensors is a cornerstone problem in fluid mechanics and experimental aerodynamics. Shu et al. (JCP 2023) established a benchmark utilizing diffusion U-Nets to reconstruct 2D Kolmogorov flow (\(Re=1000\)). However, continuous-time score-based diffusion requires dozens of stochastic sampling steps, introducing high-wavenumber dispersion errors and substantial inference latency.

In our work at the FLAME Lab (IISc), we formulated sparse turbulence reconstruction through continuous Optimal Transport Flow Matching (CNF) parameterized over a multiscale MeshGraphNets-style graph U-Net (3.2M parameters). Rather than stochastic Brownian drift, flow matching regresses straight probability paths:

$$x_t = (1 - t) x_0 + t x_1, \qquad u_t(x \mid x_1) = \frac{x_1 - (1 - \sigma_{\min})x}{1 - (1 - \sigma_{\min})t}$$

On the full 1,272-window test set from 1.56% sparse sensors, this cut reconstruction error by 43% against the published diffusion baseline (\(L_2\) error 1.008 vs 1.770) while running 1.4× faster during sampling on an RTX 4090.

Furthermore, spectral analysis revealed that the remaining physical error stemmed from numerical integrator noise concentrated at high wavenumbers (\(47\times\) excess energy at \(k \ge 96\)). By applying a late-heavy time schedule and a preconditioned residual projection, we suppressed high-frequency divergence, slashing the Navier-Stokes momentum residual \(15\times\) (from 71.9 down to 4.6) and energy-spectrum error by 64% with zero retraining.

Robotics World Models // Latency Compensation Research Project (2026)

Compensating Teleoperation Network Jitter with Latent World Models

Remote robotic teleoperation over wide-area networks suffers from variable round-trip latency, jitter, and packet loss (modeled via Gilbert-Elliott burst processes). When latency exceeds 200–300 ms, human operators destabilize, over-correcting commands and inducing destructive contact forces in manipulation tasks.

To solve this without fragile real-time frame streaming, we designed an action-conditioned latent world model (5.0M-parameter block-causal transformer) operating directly on frozen representations extracted by a self-supervised vision backbone (DINOv2-S):

$$\hat{z}_{t+k} = \mathcal{W}_\theta\Big(z_{\le t}, a_{\le t+k}\Big), \qquad z_t = \operatorname{DINOv2}(I_t)$$

The causal transformer autoregressively projects the latent visual state up to 600 ms ahead based on the operator's queued action chunk. To enable rollout training on memory-constrained GPUs (6 GB VRAM), we implemented a KV-cached autoregressive rollout that accelerated training throughput by 5.7× (3.3 → 0.58 s/step).

Benchmarking across 1,200 simulated teleoperation episodes on the PushT task demonstrated that training with multi-step rollouts cut 600 ms block-pose prediction error by ~40% compared to teacher forcing alone (8.5 vs 14.6 px), substantially beating physics-free baselines (hold-last-frame, constant velocity) during active contact phases.

LLM Systems // Replication & Financial Auditing arXiv 2412.20138 Replication

Auditing Multi-Agent Financial LLMs: Look-Ahead Bias & In-Sample Memorization

Recent academic preprints have proposed multi-agent LLM systems for autonomous financial trading, claiming superhuman annualized Sharpe ratios. We conducted an end-to-end reimplementation and rigorous ablation of the prominent TradingAgents framework (arXiv 2412.20138), deploying Qwen3-30B and Llama-3.1-8B via vLLM across dual GPUs.

Our investigation uncovered two fundamental evaluation flaws in published literature:

  • Annualization Miscalculations: A 26.6% return over a 3-month sample period was reported as 30.5% annualized, when compound geometric annualization yields approximately 165%.
  • Statistical Insufficiency: A 3-month window over 3 stocks cannot distinguish algorithmic skill from market beta and luck. In our tests, an unguided random walk baseline scored a Sharpe ratio of 3.58 on AAPL during the identical historical window.

To test for training data leakage (in-sample memorization of historical prices), we built a synthetic probe that anonymized company tickers, shifted calendar timestamps by 7 years, and rescaled price curves. While Llama-3.1-8B showed no hidden memory (12.4% error vs 4.2% for trivial last-price guessing), our ablation proved that the multi-agent debate architecture multiplied inference token costs by ~6× with marginal risk-adjusted Sharpe improvements over single-agent analysts.
