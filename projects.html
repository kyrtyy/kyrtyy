IISc FLAME Lab // Sparse-Sensor Turbulence Student Research Intern (Apr 2025 – Present)

Flow-Matching Graph Networks for Sparse-Sensor Turbulence Reconstruction

Built a flow-matching multiscale graph U-Net (3.2M parameters) that reconstructs the full \(256\times 256\) vorticity field of 2D Kolmogorov flow (\(Re=1000\)) from just 1.56% sparse sensors, replacing the diffusion U-Net of Shu et al. (JCP 2023). Cut reconstruction error by 43% against the published diffusion baseline (\(L_2\) 1.008 vs 1.770) on the full 1,272-window test set, with fewer parameters (3.2M vs 3.5M) and 1.4× faster sampling on the same RTX 4090.

Traced remaining physics error to sampler noise at high wavenumbers (\(47\times\) excess energy at \(k \ge 96\)); a late-heavy time schedule and a preconditioned residual projection cut the Navier-Stokes residual \(15\times\) (71.9 → 4.6) and energy-spectrum error by 64% with zero retraining. Rebuilt the model as a MeshGraphNets-style multiscale GNN after diagnosing permutation equivariance flaws; guarded by 153 pytest tests.

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, x_1}\left[\|v_\theta(x_t, t) - (x_1 - x_0)\|_2^2\right], \quad x_t = (1-t)x_0 + t x_1 \implies \text{NS Residual } 71.9 \longrightarrow 4.6$$

Robotics World Model // PushT Manipulation Research Project (2026)

Latency-Robust Teleoperation with Latent World Models

Built an action-conditioned latent world model (5.0M-parameter block-causal transformer) on frozen DINOv2-S features that predicts a contact-rich manipulation scene (PushT) up to 600 ms ahead to compensate for network delay in teleoperation.

Constructed a simulated teleoperation stack with a scripted operator, delayed and jittery uplink/downlink with Gilbert-Elliott burst loss, stale-command dropping, and a no-frame-leak display across 6 delay profiles (0 to 300 ms + bursts). Generated 1,200 teleoperated episodes (~710k frames) with seeded, bit-reproducible collection parallelized across a 7-GPU cluster. Added a KV-cached autoregressive rollout that accelerated training 5.7× (3.3 → 0.58 s/step), enabling multi-step rollout training on 6 GB GPUs. Training on multi-step rollouts cut 600 ms block-pose error by ~40% vs teacher forcing (8.5 vs 14.6 px), beating physics-free baselines by ~20–30% during active pushing.

$$\hat{z}_{t+k} = \mathcal{T}_\theta\Big(z_{\le t}, a_{\le t+k}\Big) \quad \longleftrightarrow \quad \text{Pose Error Cut by } \sim 40\% \text{ (8.5 vs 14.6 px)}, \quad 5.7\times \text{ KV-Cache Speedup}$$

Multi-Agent Systems // Replication & Leakage Audit arXiv 2412.20138 Replication

Multi-Agent LLM Trading: Replication, Ablation & Leakage Audit of TradingAgents

Reimplemented the TradingAgents framework: 12 model calls per decision covering 4 analysts, a bull/bear debate, a trader, and a 3-way risk debate. Executed ~21k trading decisions (~0.4B tokens) across 10 large-cap US stocks over 187 trading days, with Qwen3-30B and Llama-3.1-8B self-hosted and split across 2 GPUs via vLLM. Built a strict point-in-time data pipeline with no look-ahead (daily news, insider trades, SEC filings, split-adjusted prices) and backtested with open fills, 10 bps costs, and bootstrap confidence intervals.

Discovered two major evaluation flaws in the original paper: (1) misreporting a 26.6% 3-month return as 30.5% annualized when the true annualized return is ~165%, and (2) demonstrating that a 3-stock, 3-month test cannot separate skill from luck (random strategy achieved 3.58 Sharpe on AAPL). Designed an audit for in-sample memorization: Llama-3.1-8B had 12.4% price prediction error vs 4.2% for simple naive persistence. Measured GPU hardware energy (~3–5 kJ and ~25k tokens per decision).

$$\text{Evaluation Audit: } \text{3-Month } 26.6\% \implies \text{True Annualized } \sim 165\% \text{ (vs } 30.5\% \text{ reported in paper)}$$
